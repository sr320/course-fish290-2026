SUMMARY
This is a complete and scientifically compelling manuscript on Mytilus trossulus biomarker responses to
contaminants in Puget Sound. The study design is strong, the dataset large (n = 74 sites), and the central
argument -- that contaminant distributions and biomarker responses decouple at a regional scale -- is clearly
important. Because no text has changed from draft 01, none of the previously flagged issues have been
addressed: the Introduction lacks an explicit hypothesis, the abstract has a duplicated clause, figures are
missing, the condition-index formula is absent, and placeholder text remains in Acknowledgements, Funding,
and Data Availability. Full-pass revision is still needed before this draft is ready for journal submission.

STRENGTHS
- The funnel structure of the Introduction (broad nearshore context -> Puget Sound -> mussels as biomonitors
  -> specific biomarkers) follows the 3-component framework covered in class: define a territory, establish
  a niche, occupy the niche.
- Methods subheadings (Study Areas, Mussel Deployment, Morphometrics, Assays, Analysis) group like with
  like rather than proceeding strictly chronologically -- a good instinct from the Methods lecture.
- Results consistently report pattern + test statistic + p-value in the same sentence, which is the
  preferred format shown in the Results lecture ("Fertilized seeds were larger... mean 1.8 vs. 0.9 g,
  p=0.019; Fig. 1").
- The Discussion does not overclaim; it explicitly acknowledges that moderate correlations reflect
  buffering/acclimation rather than absence of response.

AREAS FOR IMPROVEMENT

Introduction
  - The final paragraph describes what was done but never states a directional prediction. The Week 3 lecture
    (Introduction role) and your course materials are explicit: "Define your research question, hypotheses,
    and overall approach. The more specific, the better." Add one or two sentences stating a directional
    hypothesis (e.g., "We hypothesized that P450 activity would co-vary with PAH spatial indices, while SOD
    would show weaker class-specific signal given its role as a generalized oxidative stress indicator").
    [ref: lectures/290 Week 3.pdf; readings/The Introduction Section.pdf]
  - The gap statement ("remains a critical gap") is vague. The Week 3 lecture notes that "you need to
    explain why it is important for our broader understanding to fill this gap." Name the specific gap:
    is it that existing monitoring programs measure contaminants but not biological response at scale?
    That lab-derived biomarker-contaminant relationships have not been validated at the regional field
    scale? Make the reader feel the tension.  [ref: lectures/290 Week 3.pdf; readings/The Introduction Section.pdf]
  - Paragraph 3 (WDFW / Mussel Watch program history) is background that could be condensed. The Week 3
    lecture cautions against a don't: "give a literature review on everything you know about the subject,
    then tack on your project goals at the end." The paragraph is informative but does not advance the
    gap argument; fold it into paragraph 2 or trim to two sentences.
    [ref: lectures/290 Week 3.pdf; readings/Brevity.pdf]
  - The in-text citation "Cajaraville et al. - 2000" uses a dash instead of a comma. The Week 4b lecture
    (References) specifies APA format: three or more authors use "et al." and cite year in parentheses --
    correct format is "Cajaraville et al., 2000". Check the entire manuscript for this error.
    [ref: lectures/290 Week 4b.pdf; readings/Reading Scientific Papers.pdf]
  - The title ("Assessing Mytilus trossulus response to contaminants in Puget Sound using traditional
    biomarkers") is descriptive but does not convey the key finding. The Week 2a lecture on "the art of
    the title" advises: "When in doubt, use a title that tells the main message. Many people will only
    read the title." Consider a result-forward title such as "Spatial decoupling of contaminant burden and
    biomarker responses in Mytilus trossulus across Puget Sound nearshore sites."
    [ref: lectures/290 Week 2a.pdf; readings/Ten Simple Rules Structuring Papers.pdf]

Methods
  - Figure 1 (sampling sites map) is referenced in the text but no figure is present -- only blank lines.
    The Week 4a lecture (Methods) notes that maps and site figures typically accompany site descriptions to
    help readers visualize the study area. Insert the figure or mark the placeholder explicitly as
    "[Figure 1 to be inserted: map of 74 Puget Sound sites]".
    [ref: lectures/290 Week 4a.pdf; readings/The Methods Section.pdf]
  - The condition index formula is promised ("Higgins 1938 formula (below)") but missing. The Week 4a
    lecture lists formatting rule #6: "the level of detail needed depends on your study and the customs of
    your field. Some details are trivial while others are critical." A formula used to compute a published
    response variable is critical; insert it.  [ref: lectures/290 Week 4a.pdf; readings/The Methods Section.pdf]
  - Tense is inconsistent. The Week 4a lecture lists formatting rule #1: "Use past tense in the Methods."
    "Correlation analyses use Spearman's..." should be "...used Spearman's..."; check for other present-
    tense verbs throughout this section.  [ref: lectures/290 Week 4a.pdf; readings/The Methods Section.pdf]
  - Subheading capitalization is inconsistent ("Mussel deployment and collection" vs. "Whole mussel
    morphometrics" vs. "Assays" vs. "Analysis"). The Week 4a lecture specifies "Use different font
    formatting for subheadings" and implies consistent style. Choose title case or sentence case and
    apply throughout.  [ref: lectures/290 Week 4a.pdf; readings/The Methods Section.pdf]
  - Report QA details for the assays: intra-plate and inter-plate CVs, the number of replicates retained
    after outlier removal, and blank correction. These allow readers to "judge the credibility of the
    science" -- the third function of Methods listed in both Week 4a and the course readings.
    [ref: lectures/290 Week 4a.pdf; readings/The Methods Section.pdf]

Results
  - The Moran's I values are listed as stacked inline text with inconsistent punctuation
    ("(I = 0.165, z = 3.52, p < 0.001),"). The Week 5b lecture states explicitly: "Tables are appropriate
    when the precision of a value matters (like a p-value) and you have many variables to display." This
    data is the textbook case for a table. Format as Variable | Moran's I | z-score | p-value with
    horizontal borders on header and last row only, as shown in class.
    [ref: lectures/290 Week 5b 2025.pdf; readings/The Results Section.pdf]
  - Figure 2 ("IBR.") has a one-word caption and the figure itself is absent. The Week 5a lecture covers
    figure captions and the golden rule: "Give readers the most information capable of being digested in
    the shortest time with the least ink in the smallest space possible." A caption must stand alone:
    include what was plotted, what the x/y axes represent, n, error bar type, and any statistical
    annotations.  [ref: lectures/290 Week 5a student 2025.pdf; readings/The Results Section.pdf]
  - Figure 3B is cited ("Fig. 3B") but no Figure 3 exists in the draft. Number figures sequentially and
    define every figure that is cited.  [ref: lectures/290 Week 5a student 2025.pdf; readings/The Results Section.pdf]
  - The Results section closes the Correlations subsection with an interpretive phrase: "underscoring the
    influence of contaminant mixture composition on integrated organismal responses." The Week 5b lecture
    is direct: the Results should report patterns; "very little interpretation!" Move interpretation to
    the Discussion.  [ref: lectures/290 Week 5b 2025.pdf; readings/The Results Section.pdf]
  - The LISA paragraph repeats "Superoxide dismutase activity was significant at Tulalip Bay only" and
    later "A single site, Tulalip Bay, showed significant local spatial autocorrelation for sod." The
    Week 5b lecture advises: "If everything is important then nothing is" -- consolidate to one statement
    and capitalize SOD consistently.  [ref: lectures/290 Week 5b 2025.pdf; readings/Brevity.pdf]
  - The Kruskal-Wallis statistic is reported as H = 13.72 but degrees of freedom are omitted. The Week 5b
    lecture shows the preferred format: "t = 5.57, df = 118, P < 0.001". Report H(df, N) for each test.
    [ref: lectures/290 Week 5b 2025.pdf; readings/The Results Section.pdf]

Discussion
  - The Discussion opens by restating Introduction goals rather than interpreting the strongest result.
    The Week 2b lecture describes the Discussion's mirror structure: "Begin by interpreting the data with
    respect to the specific objectives of the study. Then get progressively broader...ending with the
    concepts used to start the Introduction." Lead with your most surprising finding (the spatial
    decoupling) and interpret it before doing anything else.
    [ref: lectures/290 Week 2b.pdf; readings/The Discussion Section.pdf]
  - Limitations are scattered across the Discussion but never named explicitly as limitations. Include a
    dedicated paragraph covering: short deployment window (~85 d), single time-point sampling, potential
    temperature/food confounders on SOD at the reference site, and the IBR reference-site scaling
    assumption.  [ref: readings/Dealing with Limitations.pdf; readings/The Discussion Section.pdf]
  - Future directions are vague ("finer temporal resolution...molecular-level indicators"). Name specific
    next experiments, e.g., replicate seasonal deployments at the nine LISA-significant P450 sites, or
    transcriptomic profiling at high-IBR outlier sites. The Discussion lecture notes: the more specific,
    the more useful to the reader and the reviewer.
    [ref: lectures/290 Week 2b.pdf; readings/The Discussion Section.pdf]
  - There is no terminal conclusion sentence. End the Discussion (not Future Directions) with a single
    sentence that a reader would quote: what is the bottom line about whether traditional biomarkers
    belong in nearshore monitoring programs?  [ref: readings/The Discussion Section.pdf; readings/Scientific Writing as Storytelling.pdf]
